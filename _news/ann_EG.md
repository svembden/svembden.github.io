Last week, I had the privilege of representing Erasmus School of Economics alongside my teammates Joshua Rooijakkers, Simon Donker van Heel, and Bernhard van der Sluis. Competing against 31 top universities from across the globe, including Oxford, Cambridge, Seoul, Singapore, Harvard, and more, we tackled an incredibly challenging and timely case on predicting grid congestion in the German electricity grid — combining time series analysis and machine learning in just three intense days.

![Team picture of the Econometric Game 2025. From left to right: Simon Donker van Heel, Joshu Rooijakkers, Sem van Embden (Me), and Bernhard van der Sluis.](https://svembden.github.io/assets/img/group_pic_EG.jpg){: .img-fluid .rounded .z-depth-1 }

We are proud to share that Erasmus University Rotterdam secured 2nd place in this year’s edition, right behind the University of Warsaw, and just ahead of the University of Amsterdam. It's was an exciting battle and an honour to stand on the podium next to you!
A big shoutout as well to the Econometric Game board for organizing this event that brings together the best of international academic collaboration and applied econometrics.
